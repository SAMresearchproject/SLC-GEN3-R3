# Stewardship

SAMA is organized as a durable public-benefit knowledge surface. Its design
serves three long-horizon goals:

1. preserve enough exact provenance that later generations can distinguish
   source, derivation, execution, correction, interpretation, and approval;
2. make the mathematics traversable without disclosing unrelated private
   systems or forcing readers through the full historical workspaces; and
3. keep future editorial, legal, computational, and institutional choices
   visible instead of allowing convenience to decide them silently.

The symbolic language of Homes belongs to the wider SAM universe. This atlas
uses it only where it helps explain a mathematical or stewardship relation; it
does not turn symbolism into a technical claim.

## Public-rights floor

SAMA's [layered license](LICENSE.md) establishes a durable public-rights floor:

- `CC-BY-SA-4.0` keeps the mathematical atlas available for reading,
  teaching, translation, adaptation and redistribution while requiring
  attribution and share-alike derivatives.
- `AGPL-3.0-or-later` keeps the atlas software and network-deployed
  improvements source-available.
- Neither license may be revoked from a compliant recipient after the grant
  has been made.

That floor means the published knowledge cannot later be recalled from people
who received it under the installed terms. It also prevents a downstream
publisher from taking a modified public atlas private while continuing to
distribute it under the same licensed lineage.

## Protected implementation boundary

Public stewardship does not require indiscriminate disclosure. SAMA licenses
the mathematical exposition, interfaces, evidence routes and software that it
actually publishes. It does not grant patent rights or disclose private
physical-SLC construction details, operational know-how, counsel work,
privileged strategy or unpublished implementation internals. Those exclusions
are defined in [PUBLIC_DISCLOSURE_POLICY.md](PUBLIC_DISCLOSURE_POLICY.md).

## Institutional stewardship remains separate

Copyright licensing creates public permissions and share-forward duties. It
does not by itself assign patents, place assets in trust, bind future patent
owners, prevent separate licensing, or create a charitable/public-benefit
institution. If the physical SLC becomes protectable and operational, the
intended "out, not up" stewardship requires a separate legal structure such as
an irrevocable purpose trust, public-benefit entity, foundation, or equivalent
counsel-designed instrument holding the relevant rights.

SAMA therefore supplies the public knowledge covenant. Patent ownership,
institutional control, beneficiary duties and protection even from a future
change of mind remain a separate stewardship-law project.
