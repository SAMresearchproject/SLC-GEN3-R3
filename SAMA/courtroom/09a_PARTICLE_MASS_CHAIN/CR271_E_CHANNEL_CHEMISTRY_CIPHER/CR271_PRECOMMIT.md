## Structural Reading of the Rule

```text
WHY (a=1, b=0) is noble [He primitive case]:
  The substrate primitive ĥ itself = 2 = mirror dof at smallest level.
  Closure is self-contained at the primitive level — no external
  structure needed.

WHY (a≥1 AND b≥2) is noble [Ar, Kr, Xe, Jerroldium case]:
  ĥ-factor → mirror dof active → "shell completion" component
  d̂²-or-higher → planar carrier active → "orientational completeness"
  Both required: shell closure with full planar carrier structure.

WHY (a=0, b≥1) is reactive [pure d̂ atoms: Li, F, Co, Tl]:
  Pure 3D direction without mirror dof → orientational without
  closure → "unsatisfied" shell → forms bonds to satisfy.

WHY (a≥2, b=0) is reactive [pure ĥ^k for k≥2: O, S (sulfur)]:
  Pure mirror beyond primitive without 3D structure → can be
  partially closed but lacks d̂ orthogonal completion → reactive.

WHY mixed with low d̂ (b=1) is reactive [C, Mg]:
  Mirror + minimal 3D → 3D dof present but insufficient depth for
  noble-style closure. C with m_3 = ĥ·d̂ is the simplest mixed atom;
  Mg with R = ĥ²·d̂ has more mirror weight but still b=1.

The structural threshold is b≥2: planar carrier d̂² is the minimum
3D dof needed for noble-style closure when paired with at least one
mirror factor.
```
