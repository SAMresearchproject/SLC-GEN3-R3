# Foundation Primitives and Partition Algebra

## Conceptual abstract

The historical discovery order and the present technical dependency order are
different and both are part of the atlas. Sean Brady's antecedent relation

\[
A_0=\frac1{h\pi\,h\,d}
=\frac1{(2\pi)\cdot2\cdot3}
=\frac1{12\pi}
\]

predates the explicit naming of \(2\), \(3\) and \(\pi\) as the SAM
primitives. The typed primitive account below is the later reconstruction
that identifies the structural role of each factor; it is not presented as
the route by which the antecedent relation was first discovered.

Volume I begins with two typed primitives rather than with a gravitational
formula: a primitive distinction arity \(\alpha_H=2\) and a spatial support
dimension \(D=3\). Their first closure is

\[
D^{D-1}=\alpha_H^D+1,
\qquad
3^2=2^3+1=9.
\]

The closure does not license every integer generated later. A source-defined
five-gate diagonal grammar retains the connector alphabet

\[
\mathcal P=\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12\},
\]

and its complete route packet closes at the radix
\(R=\alpha_H^2D=12\). The next structural value, \(\Theta=18\), is not
merely a larger retained connector. It is the first overflow and is typed as a
tensor carrier. From that type transition come the exact one-eighth/seven-
eighth split and the closed \(126\), \(144\) and \(162\) sectors.

The central question of this chapter is therefore: **how does SAM travel from
two primitive assignments to a closed substrate packet while preventing
ordinary numerical resemblance from becoming false structural membership?**

## 1. Opening question and conceptual picture

The foundation has three levels, and they must be read in order.

1. **Primitive level.** \(\alpha_H\) states the arity of the primitive
   distinction; \(D\) states the dimension of spatial support.
2. **Retained-route level.** The diagonal grammar selects a finite connector
   alphabet and closes its complete packet at \(R=12\).
3. **Overflow-and-container level.** The first value outside the retained
   radix becomes a carrier, and the carrier partitions the larger closed
   sectors.

The picture is not “small integers are good.” It is a typed construction:

\[
(\alpha_H,D)
\longrightarrow
\text{primitive closure}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal P
\longrightarrow
R
\longrightarrow
\Theta
\longrightarrow
(M,N,\mathcal L).
\]

Each arrow changes what may be inferred. The primitive closure fixes the
upstream arithmetic. The diagonal selector fixes membership. The radix fixes
the retained boundary. The overflow changes type. The sector equations then
check that the carrier and retained sides recombine exactly.

## 3. Primitive closure, step by step

### 3.1 Evaluate the left side

The support dimension contributes

\[
D^{D-1}=3^{3-1}=3^2=9.
\]

### 3.2 Evaluate the right side

The primitive arity contributes

\[
\alpha_H^D+1=2^3+1=8+1=9.
\]

### 3.3 Close the two paths

Therefore

\[
D^{D-1}=\alpha_H^D+1=9.
\]

This equality is the upstream closure used to read the diagonal partition
construction. It is not a fit to a downstream observable: both sides are
evaluated entirely from the declared primitive packet. It is also not a
general identity for arbitrary \(\alpha_H,D\); the statement is the exact closure
at the registered SAM values \((2,3)\).

## 4. The diagonal connector alphabet and R12

### 4.1 Why ordinary succession is the wrong selector

The connector alphabet contains powers and mixed products of the primitive
bases \(2\) and \(3\), but membership is not “every number of the form
\(2^i3^j\)” and is not “every integer up to the next convenient base.” The
source-defined five-gate diagonal retains exactly

\[
\mathcal P=\{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12\}.
\]

This explains why numerical continuations such as \(16\), \(24\) and \(36\)
do not enter automatically. Their arithmetic existence is not evidence of a
retained connector role.

### 4.2 Derive the route radix

The complete route radix is constructed from two primitive-arity factors and
one support factor:

\[
R=\alpha_H^2D.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
R=2^2\cdot3=4\cdot3=12.
\]

The top of the accepted connector alphabet and the route kernel consequently
coincide:

\[
\max(\mathcal P)=R=12.
\]

That double role is what the native selector tests. A candidate does not win
because it is a familiar radix; it must reproduce the route kernel, partition
top and the declared write/exposure grammar together.

### 4.3 Derive the two local thresholds

The completed share is the reciprocal of the radix:

\[
A_{\rm share}=\frac1R=\frac1{12}.
\]

The side threshold resolves that share across the primitive binary arity:

\[
A_{\rm side}
=\frac{A_{\rm share}}{\alpha_H}
=\frac1{\alpha_HR}
=\frac1{2\cdot12}
=\frac1{24}.
\]

The two values are related but not synonyms: \(1/12\) is the completed
share, while \(1/24\) is one binary side of that share.

## 5. The first overflow and the closed sectors

### 5.1 Derive the overflow

The first overflow is

\[
\Theta=\alpha_HD^2.
\]

Evaluating in order,

\[
D^2=3^2=9,
\qquad
\Theta=2\cdot9=18.
\]

The important step is the type assignment after the arithmetic. Because
\(18>R\), \(\Theta\) is outside the retained register. The source packet
types it as transport/tensor carrier. It therefore must not be promoted into
an ordinary retained mass-lift row or credited as a surface-debit row.

### 5.2 Resolve the one-eighth carrier side

The binary distinction distributed across \(D=3\) support gives

\[
2^{-D}=2^{-3}=\frac18.
\]

The radix-square capacity is

\[
R^2=12^2=144.
\]

Applying the carrier fraction,

\[
R^2 2^{-D}
=144\left(\frac18\right)
=18
=\Theta.
\]

This independently reaches the same \(\Theta\) as
\(\alpha_HD^2\). The two routes close because

\[
R^2 2^{-D}
=(\alpha_H^2D)^2 2^{-D}
=12^2/8
=18
\]

at the declared primitive values.

### 5.3 Resolve the seven-eighths retained side

The complement is

\[
1-2^{-D}=1-\frac18=\frac78.
\]

Therefore

\[
M=R^2(1-2^{-D})
=144\left(\frac78\right)
=126.
\]

Since \(\Theta=18\),

\[
M=126=7\cdot18=7\Theta.
\]

The carrier and retained sides recombine without remainder:

\[
\Theta+M=18+126=144=R^2.
\]

### 5.4 Build the \(144\) and \(162\) closures

Define

\[
N=R^2=144.
\]

Then

\[
N=\frac{144}{18}\Theta=8\Theta.
\]

The face/tensor container is

\[
\mathcal F=D^{D+1}=3^4=81,
\]

and the closed ledger is

\[
\mathcal L=\alpha_H\mathcal F=2\cdot81=162=9\Theta.
\]

Removing one carrier from the nine-carrier ledger returns the radix-square
sector:

\[
\mathcal L-\Theta=162-18=144=N.
\]

The complete closure may therefore be written as

\[
\boxed{
\Theta+M=R^2=N=\mathcal L-\Theta=144
}\,.
\]

### 5.5 Keep the surface debit separate

The surface debit is

\[
\frac{D^2}{R}=\frac9{12}=\frac34.
\]

The bounce and resolved half-bounce are

\[
\frac{D^2}{2^D}=\frac98,
\qquad
\frac{D^2}{2^{D+1}}=\frac9{16}.
\]

These are exact consequences of the same primitive stack, but they are not
the \(1/8\) carrier fraction and they are not \(\Theta=18\). Numerical
combination across these types without an explicitly declared operator is a
category error.
