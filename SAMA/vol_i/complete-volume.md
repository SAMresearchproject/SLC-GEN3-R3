## Abstract

Volume I asks whether one finite substrate account can remain conceptually
coherent while supporting very different observable geometries. It begins
upstream of gravitation with primitive distinction, spatial support, a compact
closure cycle, the radix-12 register and the first carrier overflow. Those
typed objects derive the universal accumulation floor

\[
A_0=\frac1{12\pi}.
\]

Closed matter then supplies source support through the registered
matter-to-carrier interface, producing the dimensionless spherical lift

\[
A_s(r)=\frac{2GM_s}{c^2r}.
\]

The field is not itself an observable. A potential, acceleration, escape
speed, clock comparison, photon delay, coordinate-distance conversion,
strong-field landmark, cosmic inventory or halo statistic appears only when a
declared operator consumes the appropriate field object. That type discipline
is the organizing idea of the volume.

The technical route proceeds without skipping the intermediate work. It
derives weak readouts from the field; separates endpoint clocks from photon
roads; keeps invariant local \(c\) distinct from the effective
coordinate-road shorthand; constructs a global line-of-sight accumulation
separately from local source paths; carries that road through supernova and
BAO ledgers; extends the \(A\)-coordinate into exact nonrotating exterior
landmarks; derives clean cosmic inventory, the baryon-to-photon and Hubble
cascade, recombination, acoustic geometry and full CMB spectra; accumulates
many source lifts into a galaxy-halo route; and finally joins road saturation
to the clean complement in a typed cosmological-fate readout.

Failed and diagnostic routes remain part of the argument. An uncancelled local
floor, an endpoint substituted for a road, a burst law substituted for a
projection, a weak lapse extrapolated into strong field, a binding lift used
to repair recombination, two optical-depth implementation faults, a defective
peak finder, a uniform halo and a binary-control statistic all show why the
retained construction has its present form. Corrected artifacts supplement
those records rather than replacing their history.
