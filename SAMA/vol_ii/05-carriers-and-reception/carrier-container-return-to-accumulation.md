# Carrier/Container Return to Accumulation

## Opening question

> Once matter has closed as a finite material write, how does its influence
> return through carriers and containers to the accumulation field without
> turning matter back into substrate, turning \(q_A\) into measured mass,
> adding non-row operators to row counts, or hiding the source tests that left
> an exact weld or geometry open?

## Conceptual abstract

Volume II begins by separating substrate from matter. It ends by showing that
the separation is not dynamical isolation. An admitted matter row remains
matter while its influence is expressed through source support:

\[
q_A=M_{\rm obs}\left(1+\frac{|q|}{R^2}\right).
\]

That support divides exactly:

\[
T=\frac{q_A}{8},\qquad W=\frac{7q_A}{8},
\qquad T+W=q_A.
\]

\(T\) is unresolved carrier support. \(W\) is retained-write support. Neither
is measured rest mass. Ledger compression then produces the source passed to
the accumulation field

\[
A(r)=\frac{r_s}{r},
\]

after which Volume I supplies force, clock, road and strong-field readouts.
The typed route is therefore

\[
\text{closed matter}
\to q_A
\to (T,W)
\to \text{ledger compression}
\to A(r)
\to \text{readout}.
\]

QP:QP092d checks the conservation step across particle, macro and propagation
rows. LC:LC03 replays the complete matter-to-\(A\) mechanism. LC:LC06 retains
the null-conjugate witness that blocks a scalar-value-to-matter shortcut.

The return has internal addresses. CR:CR281@09a classifies exact
\(h^a d^b\) addresses as OUTPUT, FIXED POINT, TERMINAL CLOSURE or OUT.
Released \(\Theta18\) is output traffic; it is not a seated container.
The values 12, 27 and 81 are fixed points; 162 is terminal closure.

The route also preserves two informative failures. CR:CR282@09a retains A as
a non-row operator and keeps the active ledger at 162, but its axis-self weld

\[

\]

while leaving \(A\to B/\mathrm{contact}\) and direct \(A\to X1\) open.
CR:CR283@09a retains

\[

\]

but both of its precommitted row-to-face searches return zero valid

## 1. Return is relation, not category reversal

Matter admission and matter return are two different gates:

\[
\text{candidate}
\xrightarrow{\mathcal G_{\rm matter}}
\text{admitted material row}
\xrightarrow{\mathcal G_{\rm source}}
q_A.
\]

The first gate decides whether a finite closed write is matter. The second
accepts an already admitted observed-matter readout \(M_{\rm obs}\) and a
typed charge or role coordinate \(q\). SAMA-C000129-R001 records

\[
\mathcal G_{\rm source}(M_{\rm obs},q)
=M_{\rm obs}\left(1+\frac{|q|}{R^2}\right).
\]

Using the source gate as an admission rule would reverse the dependency. An
arbitrary scalar could then be assigned a \(q_A\) value and called matter.
The domain restriction prevents that.

The material source remains on the matter side of the boundary:

\[
\operatorname{type}(m)=\mathrm{MatterRow}
\]

before and after it emits

\[
\operatorname{type}(q_A)=\mathrm{SourceSupport}.
\]

No assignment

\[
\operatorname{type}(m)\leftarrow\mathrm{Substrate}
\]

occurs. SAMA-C000005-R001 is the compact route record for this distinction.

## 3. Full derivation of the matter-to-accumulation route

### 3.1 Construct source support only after admission

Given admitted \(M_{\rm obs}\) and the source-defined coordinate \(q\),

\[
q_A=M_{\rm obs}
\left(1+\frac{|q|}{R^2}\right).
\]

Because \(R^2=144\),

\[
q_A=M_{\rm obs}
\left(1+\frac{|q|}{144}\right).
\]

This is a source-support surcharge. The multiplication does not redefine
\(M_{\rm obs}\); both fields must remain serialized.

### 3.2 Split released and retained support

The substrate multiplicity is \(S=8\). Define

\[
T=\frac{q_A}{S}
\]

and

\[
W=\frac{S-1}{S}q_A.
\]

Substituting \(S=8\) gives

\[
T=\frac18q_A,
\qquad
W=\frac78q_A.
\]

Conservation follows step by step:

\[
\begin{aligned}
T+W
&=\frac18q_A+\frac78q_A\\
&=\frac{1+7}{8}q_A\\
&=\frac88q_A\\
&=q_A.
\end{aligned}
\]

The split is complementary, not duplicative. No support is created or
discarded by the decomposition.

### 3.3 Relate the split to the closed domain

For the domain itself,

\[
\Theta=\frac18R^2=\frac18\cdot144=18
\]

and

\[
M=\frac78R^2=\frac78\cdot144=126.
\]

Thus

\[
\Theta+M=18+126=144=R^2.
\]

This arithmetic mirrors \(T+W=q_A\), but the domain counts and a particular
source packet are different objects. SAMA-C000128-R001 preserves both the
complementarity and the type distinction.

### 3.4 Compress before updating the field

The carrier and retained-write channels are ledger fields. The next operator
is compression:

\[
\mathcal C_{\rm ledger}(T,W,\text{source address})
\longmapsto r_s.
\]

Only then is the accumulation field evaluated:

\[
A(r)=\frac{r_s}{r}.
\]

This separates the material/source grammar from the spatial readout. A local
force, clock comparison or road operator consumes \(A(r)\) under Volume I
rules. It does not consume raw \(q_A\) as measured rest mass.

### 3.5 A worked charged-source example

Take an admitted row with

\[
M_{\rm obs}=12,\qquad |q|=3.
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_A
&=12\left(1+\frac{3}{144}\right)\\
&=12\left(1+\frac1{48}\right)\\
&=12\cdot\frac{49}{48}\\
&=\frac{49}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The carrier share is

\[
T=\frac18\cdot\frac{49}{4}
=\frac{49}{32},
\]

and the retained write is

\[
W=\frac78\cdot\frac{49}{4}
=\frac{343}{32}.
\]

Their sum is

\[
\frac{49}{32}+\frac{343}{32}
=\frac{392}{32}
=\frac{49}{4}
=q_A.
\]

The example is exact. It does not assign the scalar \(49/4\) to a particle
mass or a container address.
