                      Finite-redshift identifiability of cosmological distance ratios

                                                         Sean Brady1
                                                 1
                                                   Independent researcher
                             (Dated: 25 September 2026 — expanded author-review manuscript)
                Finite-redshift standard-siren data cannot, without restrictions on the allowed redshift depen-
             dence, identify either the infinite-redshift plateau or the zero-redshift relative calibration of a cos-
             mological distance-ratio law. We prove two finite-window non-identifiability results. An arbitrarily
             slow component can shift the asymptotic ratio by a fixed amount while remaining uniformly small
             on any bounded redshift interval; an arbitrarily fast component can shift the extrapolated cali-
             bration while remaining uniformly small above any strictly positive minimum redshift. If positive
             decay components are bounded below by a known exponent, we derive one-sided plateau bounds
             for both decreasing (Ξ0 < 1) and increasing (Ξ0 > 1) branches. We then specialize to the Belgacem
             et al. family Ξ(z) = Ξ0 + (1 − Ξ0 )(1 + z)−n and develop exact finite-redshift reconstruction, an
             irregular-grid four-point consistency condition, covariance-aware tests, and calibration separation.
             The multiplicative calibration is identified with the relative absolute distance scale: for supernova
             reconstructions it inherits the familiar MB − 5 log10 H0 degeneracy, and for BAO reconstructions the
             rd h scale. Synthetic tests use 2–5% marginal ratio errors and a redshift-dependent non-Gaussian
             lensing-like tail. They show that realistic precision sharply limits discrimination among smooth al-
             ternatives even when the fitted plateau is biased. The result is a theorem-driven statement of what
             a finite standard-siren distance-ratio data set can and cannot establish about modified propagation
             and dark-energy phenomenology.
